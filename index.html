About

Researching how humans learn and control movement

I am a researcher working on human sensorimotor control and motor learning. My research focuses on how the nervous system learns, predicts, and adapts actions under uncertainty and changing environments.

I combine behavioral experiments, computational modeling, and virtual or altered environments to study the computational principles underlying human movement.

My research training spans bioengineering, neuroscience, and psychology, with experience at Northeastern University, Columbia University, Peking University, and Shenzhen University.

Research

Sensorimotor Adaptation

How does the nervous system learn from sensory errors?

I study the computational mechanisms underlying implicit motor adaptation, with particular interest in how visual, proprioceptive, and predictive information are integrated during learning.

Motor Control in Altered Environments

How does movement change when the physical environment changes?

I study human movement under unusual physical environments, including microgravity, to understand how the nervous system represents body dynamics and controls action when familiar physical forces are altered.

Motor Skill Learning

How do people discover efficient movement solutions?

My work examines motor variability, timing, redundancy, skill acquisition, and the transfer of learning between virtual and real-world tasks.
